Pupil mm is estimated by using average adult iris diameter (~11.7mm, anatomically stable, an established calibration technique in eye-tracking research) as a built-in ruler, then locating the pupil's dark boundary in the image via pixel-brightness analysis. This second step is experimental and hasn't been validated against real hardware — treat these mm values as a rough estimate, not a measurement.
Guided Eye Movement & Fixation Stability
Uses spoken audio cues and a tone for direction changes — not on-screen text — since you can't read the screen while looking away from it. Screens gross extraocular movement in four directions, then a brief fixation-stability check. Not a substitute for a clinical exam; cannot detect true ocular torsion (rotation) or fast (VNG-grade) nystagmus, which need equipment beyond a consumer webcam.
Turn your device sound on. Listen for the spoken direction and tone — you don't need to read anything during the test.

How it reads: a fingertip covering the rear camera and flash reflects slightly more or less light with every heartbeat as blood volume changes (green channel, which absorbs more strongly than red and gives a cleaner signal). HR and beat timing come from detecting individual pulse peaks; RMSSD and SDNN come from the variation between beat-to-beat intervals. LF/HF power comes from a full spectral (Welch/FFT) analysis of those intervals, resampled onto a uniform 4 Hz grid via cubic spline. The PLR test tracks relative iris size via on-device face-landmark detection during a brief screen-brightness flash — not clinical pupillometry. History is stored locally in this browser on this device only — not sent anywhere.
