This needs access to your phone's accelerometer and gyroscope to detect cardiac vibrations transmitted through your chest wall.

Frequency Spectrum (accel, dominant axis) — Hz
Cardiac mechanical energy should cluster as a peak roughly 0.8–2.0 Hz (48–120 bpm) with harmonics extending higher. A flat, broadband, or peak-free spectrum here means what you're seeing in the waveform above is environmental noise (traffic, fan, hand tremor), not a cardiac signal — useful to know either way.
Autocorrelation Check collecting…
A second, independent method that checks whether the waveform repeats itself at some interval — unlike the spike detector above, it doesn't require a sharp transient shape, so it can corroborate a real signal that's too smooth to trigger spike detection. Agreement between this and the spectrum badge above is real evidence; disagreement, or a low correlation score, means don't trust either alone.
Reading Stability collecting…
Transient noise (loud talking, traffic, a baby crying nearby) couples in as brief structure-borne vibration bursts that can land anywhere in the spectrum for a given window — filtering can't tell a noise burst from a real beat if both are momentary. A genuine heartbeat should hold roughly steady window to window; a reading that jumps around is the signal itself telling you the room was too noisy for that recording.

"Candidate events" are prominent discrete spikes found by time-domain peak detection — this deliberately does not yet distinguish a real cardiac transient from a tap, cough, or body shift. Treat the rate as a diagnostic, not a heart rate, until we've validated it holds steady and physiologically plausible across a longer still recording.

Consistency Score
— /100
Descriptive only, not a diagnosis. This reflects how evenly the detected beat-to-beat intervals are spaced — it does not identify, rule out, or screen for any heart rhythm condition. Natural heart rhythm is never perfectly even, and this pipeline hasn't been validated against a real ECG. If you have any concern about your heart rhythm, please talk to a doctor rather than relying on this number.
Mean Pulse Wave (ensemble-averaged) 0 beats
Every detected beat aligned on its timestamp and averaged together. Real repeating structure reinforces; random noise mostly cancels out — this is the honest way to check whether a consistent per-beat shape (comparable to published AO/AC-style SCG morphology) is actually present, rather than eyeballing a single busy raw window. Needs at least 8 beats to be worth looking at; more is better.
