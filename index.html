Frequency Spectrum (accel, dominant axis) — Hz
Cardiac mechanical energy should cluster as a peak roughly 0.8–2.0 Hz (48–120 bpm) with harmonics extending higher. A flat, broadband, or peak-free spectrum here means what you're seeing in the waveform above is environmental noise (traffic, fan, hand tremor), not a cardiac signal — useful to know either way.
Autocorrelation Check collecting…
A second, independent method that checks whether the waveform repeats itself at some interval — unlike the spike detector above, it doesn't require a sharp transient shape, so it can corroborate a real signal that's too smooth to trigger spike detection. Agreement between this and the spectrum badge above is real evidence; disagreement, or a low correlation score, means don't trust either alone.
Reading Stability collecting…
Transient noise (loud talking, traffic, a baby crying nearby) couples in as brief structure-borne vibration bursts that can land anywhere in the spectrum for a given window — filtering can't tell a noise burst from a real beat if both are momentary. A genuine heartbeat should hold roughly steady window to window; a reading that jumps around is the signal itself telling you the room was too noisy for that recording.
